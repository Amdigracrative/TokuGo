JFT-Basic · JLPT N5–N1 · Android

Learn Japanese from zero to passing JFT-Basic and the JLPT

TokuGo is an Android app for Specified Skilled Worker (SSW / Tokutei Ginou) candidates who must pass JFT-Basic, and for JLPT learners from N5 to N1: hiragana and katakana, spaced-repetition flashcards for vocabulary and kanji, grammar lessons, and practice exam sets built to look like the real tests.

What is inside TokuGo

Vocabulary

Vocabulary that actually sticks

Every card is scheduled with FSRS, a modern spaced-repetition algorithm. You rate yourself, and the app works out when the word comes back: the next interval is printed on every button. Words climb from New through Learning and Known to Mastered, and that last stage can only be reached through a quiz.

Don’t know5 minutesUnsure6 daysKnow it14 days
  1. New
  2. Learning
  3. Known
  4. Mastered
  • 95 topics in 11 groups. The Workplace group has 21 topics, from instructions from supervisors, workplace safety, schedules and contracts to factory work, construction, agriculture, restaurants, hospitality and caregiving.
  • One screen per deck. Percent complete, the status of every word, Study and Quiz buttons.
  • A pocket dictionary of 38,085 words. Search by kanji, kana, romaji or meaning.

Kanji

2,230 kanji, set by set, stroke by stroke

The kanji of each JLPT level are split into sets of about 25, ordered the way Japanese schoolchildren learn them: fewest strokes first. Every kanji has meanings, On and Kun readings with romaji, a stroke-order diagram and example words.

  • Kanji cards and quizzes with the same stages as vocabulary.
  • Kanji review gathers every due kanji from every set.
  • Write kanji with your finger: every stroke is checked for shape, direction and order.

Exam practice

Question sets shaped like the real exams

24 question sets for JFT-Basic and JLPT N5 to N1, each with the sections, question counts and time limits of the real test. Questions come with pictures, documents and announcements; the listening part is read aloud by your phone's Japanese voice.

  • Results like the official report: JFT-Basic on the 10–250 scale with the A1, A2.1 and A2.2 bands; JLPT with per-section scores and their minimums.
  • An explanation for every question after the run, including listening scripts.
  • Every set is open on the free version, with a 7-day pause per level.

Japanese basics

From zero: script, kanji basics, grammar and conversations

Can't read Japanese script yet? TokuGo's hiragana and katakana tables double as lesson lists: tap a character to hear it, see its stroke order, then take the row quiz. Kanji Basics explains how kanji work, 268 grammar lessons from N5 to N1 explain sentence patterns one at a time, and 78 short conversations put them to use in everyday situations.

  • Words you can already read in every lesson: real words made only of the characters learned so far.
  • Every grammar lesson: pattern, how to form it, examples with furigana you can listen to, then a short practice.
  • No ads on lesson and quiz screens. Hiragana, katakana, Kanji Basics, N5 grammar and the first 12 conversations are free.

Listen · speak · write

Listen, speak, write

Memorising doesn't stop at the card screen. Build audio decks from the words you choose and listen while you work, say the words you already know into the microphone, and write the kanji you know with your finger.

  • Audio decks: every word is read in Japanese, then its meaning; the player keeps going with the screen off.
  • Speaking practice: if your phone recognises the word, the next one appears by itself.
  • Kanji writing: from memory, with a faint outline, or by tapping the strokes in order.

Home & habits

Know exactly what to study today

The home screen greets you with a Japanese proverb that changes every day, a daily-goal ring and a seven-day trail. Below it: due cards, the decks you studied last, and quick practice.

  • The daily goal counts words and kanji you really learned today, not cards you looked at.
  • Reminders when cards fall due, and a daily reminder until the goal is reached.
  • A day off without losing your streak: one rest day a week is protected, and a home screen widget shows today's word, your streak and the cards due.
  • Statistics: day streak, review schedule, known words per level, exam score chart, activity map.

Frequently asked questions

Is TokuGo free?

Yes. The free version gives you 9,400 words (the top 100 of every topic), all N5 kanji, hiragana, katakana, Kanji Basics, N5 grammar, the first 12 conversations, every practice exam set with a 7-day pause per level, the dictionary and three audio decks, with ads. Premium unlocks every word and kanji, N4–N1 grammar and all conversations, removes ads, and adds offline study and exam practice without the pause.

Does it work offline?

All content ships inside the app; nothing is downloaded while you study. On the free version, starting a study session or a practice exam needs an internet connection; Premium works fully offline. Japanese audio comes from your phone's text-to-speech engine, so its voice data needs to be installed once.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no account, sign-up or password. Study records stay on your phone; to move to a new phone, export a backup file from the side panel and import it on the new one.

Which languages are supported?

The interface comes in 17 languages: Indonesian, English, Vietnamese, Nepali, Burmese, Filipino, Thai, Khmer, Mongolian, Sinhala, Bengali, Hindi, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese (Taiwan and Hong Kong), Korean and Japanese. Word and kanji meanings are complete in all of them except Japanese, which shows English meanings. Grammar lessons, conversations and exam explanations are available in 14 languages; in Simplified Chinese and Japanese they are shown in English.

Are practice scores the same as official scores?

No. TokuGo scores are an estimate based on the percentage of correct answers, for tracking progress. The official exams use a different scaled scoring, and the listening voice from your phone's text-to-speech differs between devices.

Where do the words, kanji and questions come from?

Vocabulary and kanji from JMdict and KANJIDIC (Electronic Dictionary Research and Development Group), stroke order from KanjiVG, word frequency from wordfreq. Grammar lessons and all practice questions were written for TokuGo and reviewed in several passes before release; no official questions are copied.

Which phones are supported?

Android 7.0 and up. For pronunciation and listening questions the phone needs a Japanese voice (usually from Google Text-to-speech); if it is missing, the app shows how to install one.
